# Rationale ledger

One entry per kernel mechanism. No mechanism without an entry; no entry without a removal criterion. Sources are an explicit owner requirement, an invariant, or an observed failure in the baseline (`models/decisions/2026-11-12-baseline-measurements.md`); ids such as K-3, F-16 and P-012 are those of the founding consensus and the control center's backlog. A change that adds or alters a mechanism adds or amends one entry here.

| Id | Mechanism | Source | Smallest alternative | Expected benefit | Cost | Removal criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L-01 | Change identity and the `Change: <id>` commit trailer (K-1) | Invariant: every record must hang off one change. | A branch name only. | Records, commits and measurements join without reconstruction. | One id and one trailer line per commit. | Measurement and review never read the trailer over two cycles. |
| L-02 | One `contract.xml` with eight sections and an XSD (K-2, D-2) | Owner ruling D-2; observed failure: specs of 1,400 to 5,100 words median and plans up to 16,000 that pre-write code and assertions (F-5). | A free-form Markdown brief of the change. | One readable statement of the behavioural delta that a machine can validate. | Authoring XML; one schema to maintain. | Pilot contracts regularly exceed the old spec sizes, or the owner rules for another serialization. |
| L-03 | Size and check count reported against a target, never enforced (K-2, C-2) | Observed failure: artifact size (F-5); joint condition C-2 against hard caps. | No report. | The author and owner see an oversized contract before approval. | Two numbers in `check` and the brief. | The numbers are never cited in a brief finding or an owner decision in the pilot. |
| L-04 | The independent HTML approval brief, with neutral decision statements and one legend plus color/symbol highlights for additions, modifications and removals in difference briefs (K-3, D-3) | Explicit brief requirement (F-15); observed failure P-012, approval of long artifacts on memory and a skim; C-0007 D13 requires neutral decisions and highlighted differences without repeated status labels. | Read the contract directly, or scan conversational attribution and repeated status labels. | Decision makers identify changed scope and tradeoffs without assuming who a conversational pronoun names or rereading labels on every item. | One independent pass and one record per revision; neutral paraphrase, semantic comparison, template styling and symbols/print styles. Formal approval provenance stays separate; no new kernel parser or validation mechanism. | Remove the brief if it adds no information at pilot sizes and fidelity checking finds no decision that would otherwise be missed; remove the visual convention if it obscures rather than clarifies changed decisions over two cycles. |
| L-05 | The approval record as a decision bound to the contract and check hashes (K-3, C-3) | Invariant: the owner approves alone; an agent-editable status field is not authority. Owner ruling of 2026-11-13: an approval authorizes execution; a hold is scheduling, never a kernel state. Owner requirement of 2026-11-14: the record holds the decision, not the conversation; `Source` is removed from new records and `Statement` is `approved` or the rejection reason. | A status attribute on the contract. | Approval is attributable and bound to exact text, and authorizes execution without a second decision, while committing no conversational detail. | One record; two hashes copied from `check`. | Approval moves to a system that authenticates the owner, making the decision record redundant. |
| L-06 | Material revision as a new approved revision with a difference-only brief; mutable `notes.md` (K-4, D-5) | Observed failure: 17 of 41 changes widened scope after approval, half of the admitted files being tests of in-scope files (F-8). | Re-approve the whole contract on any edit. | Tests and sequencing need no ceremony; real boundary changes get a short, explicit owner decision. | A revision counter and a second brief when it happens. | Pilot revisions are all trivial or all rejected, showing the gate adds no decision. |
| L-07 | Standalone execution uses one change per worktree; a serial orchestrated batch of approved changes permits parked changes with one active implementer, retaining each start revision, pre-existing-work identification and complete-diff review (K-5) | Observed failure: 6 committed out-of-scope files in a month and noisy window-based ownership (F-8); explicit owner-approved serial parked-change policy in C-0007 and its review F1, whose rehearsal disproves the claim that each complete diff contains only one change. | The history engine of GRACE 4, or a worktree per change for every batch. | Standalone work retains worktree isolation; a serial batch prevents simultaneous implementers while exposing all later commits to each parked change's review, without pretending that its diff belongs only to it. | One start per change; standalone worktrees or serial-batch commit attribution and review of other changes' paths; later source commits stale evidence, and even another approved change cannot waive a parked change's Protected paths. | Recorded serial-batch isolation failures cannot be resolved through boundaries, attributed commits and full-diff review: retire the shared-worktree exception or use owner-authorized isolated workspaces; admit a broader mechanism only through its standing admission test. |
| L-08 | Protected paths that block at close (K-5) | Invariant: a boundary the owner approved must be able to refuse. | Review alone. | A forbidden write cannot close unnoticed. | One glob match over the diff. | No contract in the pilot declares one. |
| L-09 | Run receipts with source identity and the candidate rule, which excludes the kernel-managed paths, `.grace5/` and the installed `grace5-*` skill directories, and nothing else (K-6) | Observed failure: evidence labels that prove nothing (F-10) and stale evidence at archive. | Paste command output into the conversation. | Evidence belongs to named inputs; record writes never force reruns. | One receipt per run; one tree hash. The exclusion of `.grace5/changes/` is by path, so a file a check executes from there is bound by nothing (`source-identity.md`). | Reviews and closes never consult receipts over two cycles. |
| L-10 | Automated checks as project commands claiming command completion only (K-6, C-4) | Observed failure: literal assertions satisfied by the agent that wrote them (F-5, F-10). | Text assertions. | Evidence is the project's own gate, labelled honestly. | Gate wall clock, which any method pays (F-11). | A pilot repository has no command worth running as a gate, so every criterion rests on trace or manual evidence. |
| L-11 | Trace checks over ordered log markers with recorded provenance (K-12) | Owner request of 2026-11-12; observed failure: the device channel first found 34% of firmware defects with only notes as evidence (F-16). | The manual observation of L-12. | Executed, repeatable evidence for behaviour that gates cannot see. | Marker lists to maintain; a capture command per project. | No voluntary use beyond the required demonstration, or a maintenance and false-failure burden the owner judges unacceptable against the time saved. |
| L-12 | Manual checks satisfied only by an attributable observation (K-7) | Observed failure: planned procedures counted as results; 438 scenarios without executable evidence (P-002). | A free-text note in the review. | A manual obligation is approved up front and closes only on a recorded pass for the candidate. | One command per observation. | Every pilot manual check is replaced by an automated or trace check. |
| L-13 | Independent review record with findings, adequacy and rule verdicts (K-8) | Observed: review first found 57% of classified defects (F-2). | Self-review only. | The channel that finds most defects leaves a record that close can verify. | One reviewer pass per candidate. | Over two cycles review first finds no defect that a check or the owner had not already found. |
| L-14 | `status` as the one whole-change read, strict at the gate and partial elsewhere (K-9) | Observed: `grace status` ran 540 times; one invalid artifact darkened every lookup. | Read the records by hand. | One answer to "what is the next obligation". | One command. | Agents and owner stop calling it. |
| L-15 | `close` validating verdicts on the final candidate (K-13) | Invariant: closure by record existence closes failed and stale evidence. | A checklist in the execute skill. | A change cannot close on a failed, stale, replayed or unreviewed state. | The rules of `evidence-records.md`. Closed still means `close.xml` exists: the kernel authenticates no record, and a hand-written one is caught by review and measurement, not by `status` (`evidence-records.md`). | Over two cycles `close` never refuses anything that the execute skill's own reading of `status` had not already stopped. |
| L-16 | `check` structural validation against the XSDs | Invariant: a contract a machine executes must be well formed before the owner reads its brief. | Validation inside `run` only. | Malformed contracts and criteria without checks are caught before approval. | One command. | Its findings never change a contract in the pilot. |
| L-17 | `init` that links existing project documents and overwrites nothing (G-8, G-13) | Observed: projects keep their own standing-truth registries outside the framework (F-12). | A README instruction. | Adoption without a context corpus. | One command. | Projects create the directory by hand without loss. |
| L-18 | Three skills: prepare, execute, review (G-13) | Observed: six of sixteen skills were ever invoked (F-4); framework text grew 2.3 times in five months (F-9). | One skill. | Each lifecycle role states each rule once. | Three texts to keep in step with the CLI. | A skill has no invocation over two cycles. |
| L-19 | Wire-boundary technique in the method document (K-10) | Observed failure: wire mismatches across components (P-004). | Nothing. | Both sides are checked against one definition without a framework registry. | One section of prose. | No pilot change crosses a boundary. |
| L-20 | Records structured for measurement (K-11) | Owner requirement: the successor evolves under measurement (F-14). | Transcript parsing. | M2 reads records directly. | Schema discipline. | The control center stops measuring. |
| L-21 | This ledger and the evolution rule (I-5) | Agreed design bet I-5; observed growth of the old framework (F-9). | Commit messages. | Every mechanism carries its exit condition. | One row per mechanism. | The owner stops evaluating mechanisms against their criteria for two cycles; the ledger is then dead text and goes. |
| L-22 | `close` moves the change directory under `.grace5/changes/closed/`; commands resolve both locations and the listing counts the closed changes (`commands.md`) | Explicit owner requirement of 2026-11-13: closed changes must leave the open ones in view. | The listing hides closed changes without moving them. | The open changes are the directory listing and the status listing, in a repository that closes dozens of changes a month. | One move at close; two locations to resolve. | Over two cycles no pilot repository exceeds one screen of changes, or the owner reports the split adds nothing. |
| L-23 | The version pin `.grace5/kernel-version`, outside the candidate, and `grace5 upgrade`, which composes the migration steps from the pinned version to the kernel's version, refuses only the open changes a step declares it would write, and checks every change's hashes afterwards (`upgrade.md`) | Explicit owner requirement of 2026-11-13: taking a kernel version is a kernel facility, from X to X+n, needing nothing outside the kernel; owner ruling of the same day, after a pilot with three open changes was locked out, that open changes no step touches do not block an upgrade. | A written procedure in a skill, carried out by an agent by hand. | A repository takes a version with three commands and a commit; a kernel never operates on state laid out for another version; the state's version is read by a command, not by a person; a change may span kernel versions. | One file in the state; one step per version boundary that changes the layout, the local ignore file or the skills, shipped with the release that carries it, each declaring the change ids it would write; the upgrade verifier run on a clone at every release, once with an open change beside the closed ones. | Over two cycles no kernel release changes the layout, the ignore lines or the skills, so every chain is a pin bump; or the owner moves pilots to a packaged kernel whose installer carries the migration. |
| L-24 | `grace5-orchestrate` coordinates approved changes through implementer and independent reviewer subagents, parks critical stops, commits resumable ledgers under `.grace5/orchestration/runs/`, and every role reads optional prose instructions in `.grace5/extensions/`; the 0.5.0 to 0.6.0 step installs the skill beside installed packaged skills | Explicit owner requirement of 2026-11-13: ship a reusable orchestrator out of the box, mostly unattended, escalating only critical issues and sharing repository-local instructions across roles. | Ad hoc session instructions for each batch and each role. | A fresh session runs independent approved changes despite parked owner gates, and local instructions are written once without product-specific kernel integrations. | One skill, one sentence per lifecycle skill, two optional state directories outside the reviewed source diff, a migration addition and a manual rehearsal; extension authority relies on the reader. | Over two cycles no pilot uses the orchestrator for multiple changes, or the owner finds its escalations and ledger cost exceed the coordination saved; remove the orchestrator and ledgers then. Remove the extensions convention if no pilot shares one instruction across roles over the same period. |
| L-25 | A version marks an owner-requested release, not a change: any number of changes close under one version, and only a release change bumps `package.json`, the README status line, the pin and the migration table (`upgrade.md`) | Explicit owner requirement of 2026-11-14: creating and installing a version is an explicit owner request, and one version carries many changes, not every issue fixed. | Bump the version on every change, as every change did until now. | Releases are meaningful and rare; no version or pin churn per change; a version names a stable state across all the changes it carries. | One release change per version, which collects the steps its batched changes need. | The owner asks for a version per change again, or releases become so rare that a version and its migration table drift apart. |
